## Lessons this week
* **First real stress test.** The S&P fell 2.7% on the week, including -4.4% on Thu 5 Nov. Every account
  moved by about its stock exposure: 3x accounts 3.2x the S&P, 2x accounts 2.1x, 60/40 0.5x. The small
  extra beyond the nominal leverage is the daily-reset drag and financing of the leveraged funds, as
  modelled. No behaviour mismatch, so no cost-model bug.
* **Diversification did its job.** The multi-asset accounts (half or one third outside US stocks)
  fell 30-40% less than the champion on the sell-off: the safety-for-growth trade the backtests showed.
* **Data, not markets, caused the week's worst numbers.** (1) The S&P stand-in used the Dow while FRED
  lagged, and the Dow moved the other way on 2 and 3 Nov: now SPY is used first. (2) Real-ETF prices
  skipped a finished session after midnight UTC: fixed to New York time. (3) MTUM's 4-for-1 split
  arrived in the feed before its adjustment (-77% in a day): a data guard now pauses the account, and
  the shares are rescaled when the feed re-bases. Recorded history was kept, except one documented
  rollback of a single feed-error day.
* **Ranking vs the backtest.** Lower-risk accounts lead after two weeks only because the market fell;
  the leaderboard's ordering is about decades. No champion change on real-time results.
* **Signals close to flipping.** The S&P is 1.0% above its 10-month average; a November close below it
  would cut the champion's trend sleeve on 1 Dec. The month-end-close whipsaw found in Exp 34 is the risk.
